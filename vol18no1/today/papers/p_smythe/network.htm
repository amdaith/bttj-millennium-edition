A History of Mobile Communications: 1995 to 2010

Network Changes

One of the biggest changes for the cellular network was the rapid change from voice to data. By the early 1995 less than 1% of the traffic on a cellular network was data. The graph below shows how mobile access to Email changed this so that by the year 2002 half the traffic was data.

Fig 2. What happens if people use mobile terminals for internet?

By year 2001 many people who were using powerful multimedia terminals were finding that even 64kbit/s access was very slow for broadband multimedia. Yet third generation mobile networks were still 2 years away from deployment and the original hopes of providing 2Mbit/s access had been dropped to 144 kbit/s due to many of the network problems of deployment. Nevertheless broadband mobile access was available for most asymmetric systems by using spare capacity on Digital Audio Broadcasts, 1.7Mbit/s, and Digital TV, 20 Mbit/s. The GSM link was used to request downloading and payment while the information was queued and delivered to the users terminal. All this was handle by the terminal agent. One of its key features, as we know today, was its ability to "hand over " the application to the nearest fixed network where xDSL technologies provide multi-Mbit/s access rates.

By 2005 most telephone kiosks had developed into public information points where people could both download and transmit information at bit rates of up to 25 Mbit/s. The mobility management of this was handle by mobile IP in IPv6. That way people could move their mobile terminal anywhere in the world and to them it would appear like their desktop terminal.

Fig 3. Information 'Petrol Station'

The agent technology also changed the way this heterogeneous, non-uniform environment was exploited. Now agents were busy all day seeking out and exchanging information. Networks, far from managing the load, became permanently loaded. The agents used dynamic pricing to buy capacity when it was cheap to trade information, but to relinquish it to more premium point-to-point or urgent services. It became unthinkable to waste capacity just because a cumbersome management or inflexible billing system stopped its utilisation
