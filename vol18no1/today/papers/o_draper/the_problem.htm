Universal Content - Extracting the Essence

The Problem

As users become more aware of what information technology is making possible, they look for more and more information services to be delivered to their terminal device, be this a desktop PC or a network appliance [1], such as a laptop PC, television, PDA or mobile phone. A service that can provide information that has been tailored to meet the user's specific requirements (e.g. look and feel of the original). The service will result in significant commercial benefits for the content provider , whilst retaining the core information contained in the content and the presentation. Designing a robust, scalable system that meets the requirements for both enduser and service provider is the challenge for Universal Content (UC).

Currently multimedia Web content is authored with the personal computer as the target client device. This gives a service provider an opportunity to adapt the content for network devices. These devices have markedly different characteristics to a typical PC, notably screen size and resolution, colour depth, processing power, memory and storage [1].

Fig 1. Content re-purposing in the UC system

We are working from the principle that people will have different requirements as they access the same source of content, dependent upon whether they are accessing this information from their workplace, their home or from a mobile connection. Whatever the users current circumstances, UC aims to automatically provide the user with appropriate and accurate information. In this way subscribers to UC could access a Website supplying content in the form of a weather forecast. They would receive the content in differing format according to their device and network capabilities, i.e. client profile. On a multimedia PC with fast network connection this content might take the form of a video clip giving the forecast, on a laptop with a slower network connection this forecast might take the form of a series of still-images, and on a mobile device a text weather synopsis could be received. Another enhancement that the UC system could provide is to enhance Web pages used by a client suffering from colour-blindness. This can be achieved by changing the colours of the background, text and links so as to provide optimum visibility to the client.

By using, our system content providers are able to ensure that the key components of their information can be accessed by their clients in some format without the additional overhead associated with replicating and managing extra copies of source content. Whilst currently it is an option to provide alternative content for differing user profiles, there is a considerable manual effort required in managing multiple-version content which may become an almost impossible task in large server systems.

In the UC system we are proposing the content provider would supply one set of media content with associated metadata tags. These tags would subsequently be used by the UC system to automatically provide re-purposed content appropriate for each client profile.
