Lifestyle Monitoring for Supported Independence

Authors

Nicholas Edwards

Nicholas Edwards graduated in 1990 with first class Honours in Natural Sciences from Jesus College Cambridge. After completing a DPhil and Postdoctoral Research Fellowship in Laser Physics at Oxford University, he joined BT in 1995. For nearly four years, Nicholas worked in a team creating new applications for communications in healthcare. In particular, he managed a project developing new Telecare services to enhance independence and quality of life for older people living in the community. He now works in the Business Technology Analysis Unit, part of Advanced Communications Engineering on a project developing a new communications Application Programming Interface.

 

Nigel Barnes

Nigel Barnes works within Advanced Communications Engineering (ACE) researching new applications and the use of technology. Nigel joined BT as an apprentice in September 1986. After completing his apprenticeship, during which time he gained the BTEC NC and HNC in Electrical and Electronic Engineering and Communications at Suffolk College, Nigel became a technician in the field of optical communications research, developing parallel opto-electronic interconnect and opto-electronic neural network demonstrators. In 1991 Nigel chose to further his education at the University of Kent at Canterbury where he attained a first class BEng(Hons) in Communications Engineering in 1994. On returning to BT at Adastral Park, Nigel joined the Technology Research Unit and was involved in the development of optical communication system demonstrators for European collaborative projects. In 1996 Nigel moved from optical research to applications research in the field of Telemedicine. Nigel has been heavily involved in BT's Telecare Research Project and the CAREnet Lifestyle Monitoring Trial, currently he is researching applications in the area of 'smart homes'.

 

Paul Garner

Paul Garner joined BT at Adastral Park in 1984 after graduating from Hull University with a BSc. in Applied Physics. Initially he worked on the development and production of optical receivers for Trans-Atlantic telecommunication systems. The work of this team was recognised by the Queens Award for Technological Achievement in 1989. His work on optical devices later broadened to include specialised optical fibre amplifiers for TV systems. In 1990, he was awarded an MSc. in Laser Physics. In 1993, Paul moved to the Telemedicine research team at Adastral Park, where he was responsible for managing the BT Telemedicine trials. Since 1996, he has lead the Telematics group, researching both Telemedicine and Telecare applications. Paul has published over 20 journal papers in the field of telecommunication applications. Paul is a Member of the Institute of Physics and a Chartered Physicist.

 

Duncan Rose

Duncan Rose joined BT Laboratories in 1988. He worked initially on data modulator and demodulator DSP coding techniques and developed an ultra-high-speed digital equaliser for use in satellite systems. He has also investigated the application of signal processing techniques for the benefit of hearing impaired telephony users. In 1994 he developed telepresence systems to increase the realism of videoconferencing systems by the provision of eye-to-eye contact. In 1996 he was awarded a MSc in Telecommunications Engineering. Since 1997 he has also been working extensively on the BT CAREnet project, a research project exploring the application of communications technology for supporting older people in the community.
