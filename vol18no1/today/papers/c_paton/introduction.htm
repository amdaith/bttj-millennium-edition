Process Engineering for the Alliance Partners

Introduction

In the telecommunications industry, as in many other industries, documented processes are required in order to ensure that a business can carry out its functions in an efficient, cost-effective manner. A Business Process Model consists of the complete set of that business's processes, in a format such that the relationship between different processes may be clearly seen, and the impact of any organisational or functional changes within the business may be tracked through all its processes. It should also facilitate the production of new processes, in such a way that the development and implementation costs are kept to a minimum.

Historically, BT's business was focussed around a dominant product line, the Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN) service. As a result, its business processes were also centred on PSTN, and as other services were developed, the supporting processes and systems were developed on an ad hoc basis, as an add-on to the existing PSTN business. Until recently, little work has been put into developing an integrated business model. The results of this approach can be seen today, where our processes are fragmented, and process developments to support new services tend to be treated as stand-alone activities, often divorced from the Operational Support System (OSS) developments and the technology being used. It has also led to considerable duplication of data in systems and mis-matches for the same data types across systems. Now that BT's business is no longer focussed on a dominant service but rather on providing integrated solutions to its customers' communications needs, this approach is no longer tenable.

In an integrated business model, process, OSS development and technology should be viewed as a single activity, which we refer to as Operational Design. Each of these components can be thought of as an integral part of a triangle (figure 1) with the lack of any one or more components resulting in an incomplete solution.

OSS development costs often form a large part of the overall costs of launching a new service or network technology. This is because OSS development tends to happen in parallel or before an end-to-end process has been developed. In many cases, some of these costs can be avoided by paying attention to the fact that the primary purpose of an OSS is to support one or more processes. Thus by taking a holistic view of process and systems right across the business's core functions, we can reduce both development costs and time to market for a wide range of services and technologies.

The task of this team was to come up with an approach that would enable some of these problems to be tackled pragmatically.
