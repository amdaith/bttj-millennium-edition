The Future of the Phone Box

Inside the box

Many of the services discussed so far do not need the user to be inside the box, just nearby. The box may seem fairly empty. Most functionality could be provided by vandal-resistant voice or visual interfaces, with no need for any slots that might have sauce sachets inserted. Cash could be replaced by either smart cards or by iris scan identification. An anti-noise environment could allow loud-speaking telephones to be used without the problems of traffic noise drowning out the conversation, while ensuring privacy. With no handset to cut off, nothing obvious to break, and no cash to steal, there would be less attraction for vandals. With built in video surveillance, even graffiti will be deterred.

Secure services

Electronic cash needs security, so digital signatures and encryption can be assumed in all its forms except for the smallest of transactions. A third component of security is authentication of identity. An iris-scanning unit in phone boxes might be of great assistance for a wide range of other secure services. For instance, buying tickets, downloading electronic cash on to a smart card, buying and downloading information products, or even printing off photographic identification could all be done in future phone boxes. Of course, these services all rely on appropriate security during iris scan registration.

 

Fig 3. An example of iris scanner

The high quality printing that would be needed for ticketing and so on would perhaps be too expensive for most phone boxes, but might be appropriate for a more deluxe offering in strategic locations. Housing such equipment might seem to make such boxes an attractive target for vandals but this threat would presumably be diminished by the knowledge that any vandal would appear on high-quality video, and even if wearing a mask, an iris scan would make good evidence.

Maps, vouchers and shopping

The benefits of these boxes may well offset these potential problems. As well as printing tickets, such boxes could provide local maps, personalised according to what the recipient is interested in, complete with vouchers for local shops. If the customer tells the phone what he is looking for, an agent could electronically shop around and find which local stores have the item with the best deal, or even arrange to buy it from the net. In this way, the phone box could act as a shopping terminal, spanning both electronic and physical shops. When it has done the shopping around, it could print off a map and directions, highlighting the places of interest. If the customer has an electronic navigation device, the box may communicate with this and download appropriate information. This would allow us to provide a personal shopper service too, continuing the service via a mobile device long after the customer has left the box.

This could be much more lucrative than it might initially sound. It is not simply a matter of directing people to the nearest appropriate retailer, though this might sometimes be all that is needed. By making such directions, BT is put firmly into the value chain and might at the very least attract commission from the retailer. But the phone box could really be just the front-line terminal giving access to a complete shopping service that could be offered as a BT service. This could harness a database with the entire product offerings and terms from every supplier. We could thus put customers in contact with appropriate suppliers directly, finding customers a good deal and ensuring healthy competition between suppliers. Of course, BT could attract a commission from each of these sales. Furthermore, we could leverage automated logistics systems to run a virtual distribution business too. We could arrange collection and delivery at an appropriate time for the customer by linking together warehousing and transportation services, while minimising costs by means of efficient algorithms. Becoming involved in so much of the value chain could put us in a powerful and lucrative position. This 'one-stop-shop' service could put BT centrally in the customer's focus, where our brand becomes much more elastic, a completely cross-industry lifestyle brand for both domestic and business customers. BT could become the source of not just telecomms, but of anything that can be arranged by using any form of IT. And all starting in the phone box.

People could shop for information products in phone boxes. They could download MP3 music (or any future standard), maybe even movies. This would need a very high speed connection if long sessions are to be avoided, though the recipient would not necessarily need to be in the box for the duration of a download.

In-box video services

If the box can be made more secure by using such technologies as video surveillance and iris identification, then perhaps video services could be incorporated in most places. With display technology plummeting in cost, even a holo-deck environment would not be impossible. The floor could actually be the top of a rolling ball that tracks the movement of the occupant while allowing them to walk as far as they like in any direction, immersed in a fully three dimensional virtual environment. With virtual meetings, video-conferencing, or pure fantasy tele-travel, using the phone might be the least of the services these boxes would get used for.

The video display could offer talking-head-style interfaces, adjusted automatically for the language of the occupant, perhaps even for their culture. Voice and gesture recognition and natural language processing, including translation should be adequate in about ten years.

Email and Dial-up services

It would be possible to provide Internet services in phone boxes, for many people who do not have access from home. Some terminals in airports already allow customers to send emails after inserting a credit card. People might occasionally need to access the wider Internet and a phone box might be a convenient location for such a service. For some people who live next to a phone box, collecting their email from the box could be almost as easy as lifting letters off the doormat.

Printing and scanning

The advanced audiovisual interface will allow the emulation of virtual machines, so the box could essentially replicate any sort of terminal, including all the features available on the latest Touchpoints. With the inclusion of some means of providing high quality hard prints, the boxes could offer ticketing, photo printing, perhaps even a wide selection of traditionally post office services. Scanning technology would even further enhance their capability, allowing transfer of physical pictures.

Power supply

Phone boxes might be able to provide power to portable devices. Although unfeasible with current technologies, it may be possible with some future battery technologies that they can be charged very quickly, and this could be another useful potential service. Sometimes, users might have a long session, or use services that consume too much battery power. Having a power supply available would be a very useful facility. Perhaps it could offer a range of voltages and connector types.

Advertising

Finally, the screens may be used for advertising purposes, and this could offset some of the cost of the calls being made. These could of course be personalised to the interest of the user. It may even be possible to have video advertisements on the outside of the box. Using lenticular screens, and depending on the maturity of identification technology, different adverts could be seen by different passers-by. These screens would also allow people to preview video games or movies before downloading them on to their mobile devices at high speed.

Environmental friendliness

More environmentally pleasing, the same facility would allow the boxes to be camouflaged, blending in well with the local environment. Smart paints that change according to their environment would serve equally well. Phone boxes have already changed from red to grey, soon they may be virtually invisible except for a discreet beacon.

Other services

Oh, and the box might include a phone too. This might have changed from black to grey. And a seat would be nice.
