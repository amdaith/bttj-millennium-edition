The Future of the Phone Box

Outside the box

Urban positioning

A common characteristic of phone boxes is that they do not move around. Many are also located at good positions in town centres. These boxes could host a local positioning system. Based on radio, radar, video or ultrasound devices, they could provide the means for positioning within millimetres, perhaps in conjunction with other devices on lampposts or walls. Low powered devices on lampposts would be assured of a constant optical power source, but of course, some of the devices could be positioned at the box itself. Together with these other local sensors, it could maintain a map of the vicinity that is updated every second or even more frequently, so that the map can include the positions of all the moving objects such as people and cars in the area.

There are many uses for such dynamic local positioning and mapping. For example, blind people could walk around with audio signals telling them exactly where they are and warning them of people or other obstacles nearby. They could easily find their way around, avoiding collisions, and have a smart system providing them with appropriate information wherever they go. This information could be provided either from a remote system over a telecomms link via the phone box, or from store information systems as they wander around. As they wander through a shop, they could be guided to exactly the place they want to get to, and be informed about the goods that they are interested in. These information services could be valuable to sighted people too of course, allowing personalised information and advertising to each shopper, according to exactly where they are. A portable electronic map would enable navigation much more accurately than is possible currently with GPS. Linking these urban positioning systems to mobile terminals would allow a wide range of navigation, social, business and tourist information to be offered.

These positioning systems have a safety benefit too. Being able to pinpoint a problem would speed up the response of emergency services. When a person pushes the distress button on their mobile, the services would know exactly where they were, not just to the nearest hundred metres. Automated 'care in the community' systems in a hospital could monitor a person's activities, recognising much more quickly when unusual behaviour indicates a potential problem.

Positioning systems could facilitate everyday services such as taxis. A simple button in the box could be linked to a local taxi agent and call a taxi automatically to the box. Of course, the taxi company might choose to give regular customers mobile devices that call a taxi to wherever they are, using the same system. It is perhaps unlikely that the box would need to provide a radio beacon for this purpose, but there may be a few novel services that might require such a facility.

Certainly, being able to broadcast an exact location might make it easier for people to use phone boxes as meeting points. If a child gets lost and goes to a box and pushes the appropriate distress button, its parents could be automatically called and given automated directions to the box. Eventually, identification could be iris based, but in the near future the child might simply carry a card with the appropriate numbers stored on it. The cellular system would be able to pinpoint the location of the mobile phones in order to provide accurate directions.

As we write this article, prototype flying cars have just been announced. Their mass-market success would seem unlikely, but we might eventually see a few for specialist purposes or for the rich. If so, these will need very sophisticated navigation and positioning systems to avoid bumping into things and of course, each other! Satellites will almost certainly play the major part in this, but phone boxes might be able to play a minor role too.

Electronic cash exchange

Electronic cash will become commonplace sooner or later. Various forms may exist in parallel, some based on smart cards, others in networked accounts, others as certificates on mobile computers or other devices. Interchange between these various forms, and indeed between people, may sometimes need special terminals. Phone boxes may be able to provide this functionality. Currency could be exchanged, and cash moved between devices and people. The phone box might even offer electronic banking services. Of course, with the various zones around a box, different types of transaction might require degrees of proximity to the box.

Other obvious services in this category are topping up smart cards or buying time on mobile phones (the box may accept a range of payment options).

Radio LAN capability

People are expected to carry round a wide range of electronic devices on their person in the future. Some of these may communicate with each other via the skin, which offers a high capacity medium, but perhaps some will need a more conventional interface. This could be offered in the phone box, which could create a nano-LAN to link together and interpret protocols between the machines as required.

The phone box, with its multiple zone capability, could provide a LAN to link together any devices in its area. People sitting on a nearby bench might be able to network their equipment this way, although of course, most portable equipment should be able to set up communications with other devices directly. The speed of connection would of course vary according to distance from the box. The phone box could provide protocol conversion capability to allow incompatible devices to communicate via a bilingual intermediary across the network.

Depending on nearby obstructions and buildings, it may also be feasible to offer radio LANs for nearby buildings, capturing some of their internal communications markets. Providing secure radio lobes for this purpose should be possible.

Security tagging support

Shoplifting remains a large problem in cities. Tagging is becoming a more popular means of controlling the activities of offenders instead of imprisonment. Phone boxes could play a role in detecting the presence of tagged offenders and monitoring where they go. Nearby shops could be warned so that they can monitor their behaviour closely. The video cameras in the phone box might be able to send up-to-date pictures instead of just a stored photo. This would make disguises much less effective.

As well as tagging shoplifters and other criminals, expensive goods could be tracked too. A thief may manage to remove it from the shop and begin an escape, but still have his movements tracked by means of chips in the goods.

Entertainment

With a large number of channels available on digital TV, it is expected that some of these might be used for live coverage of interesting events in city centres. Cameras may monitor traffic or weather, in fact anything that people are interested in, and relay it to the viewer. In the shoplifting example, perhaps video cameras could zoom in on the escapee. Apart from assisting in criminal apprehension, this could provide valuable real-time entertainment. Certainly, there is no reason for phone box cameras to be idle. When they are not being used for a higher purpose, they could be remotely controlled by remote viewers on digital TV or the Internet.

Of course, electronic cash management, information, or software purchases, or electronic shopping could be feasible in such a box. Or people might 'fill up' a portable entertainment device before proceeding on their journey.

Emergency support

The box could include emergency buttons that call the appropriate services to the box, or enable lost children to be re-united with their parents. However, if a loudspeaker were included in the box, it would now be able to give audio assistance to people within earshot. If a medical emergency occurs nearby, the speaker could relay advice to nearby people until the emergency services arrive, such as first aid instructions. A video camera in the box would help greatly for the remote experts to see what the problem is, and directional microphones may allow both-way audio communications too.

Phone boxes with such speakers would make good PA alarm stations too.
