The Multilingual World Wide Web

Introduction

During the year 2000, non-English speaking World Wide Web users will outnumber English speaking users. Further, the number of non-English users of the World Wide Web is growing at a far greater rate than the number of English speakers (figure 1). Particularly fast growth is seen in Brazil, China and Korea.

Providers of Internet-based services will have to become multilingual and multicultural if they are to capitalise on this huge increase in potential market.

Internet portal providers are among the first to recognise the value that can be added their services by providing facilities, such as automatic translation, to help users cope with information in other languages. For example, AltaVista (http://www.altavista.com/) now allows automatic translation of Web pages using the Systran (http://www.systransoft.com) translation system [1], and Yahoo (http://www.yahoo.com) also allows translation using the Logos (http://www.logos.com) translation system.

Making a service multilingual may involve much more than just translating some of the text. In many cases, services would benefit from being ported to other locales, but the cost is prohibitive because the system was designed originally for monolingual use, and it is now too expensive to re-engineer it. For example, mistaken choice of character encoding scheme at an early stage could make a significant difference to the cost of porting to other locales.

The main aim of this paper is to show how choice of language affects the various components that might comprise a Web-based service, and to present the technologies that are available to provide solutions.
