Towards Human-Centred Intelligent Systems: The Intelligent Assistant

Authors

David Djian

David Djian received an engineering degree in Computer Science from the Institut d'Informatique d'Entreprise in France. He then did an MSc project at Oxford University Robotics Research Group on logical sensors for robots. He received an MSc in Robotics and Vision from the University of Nice working on the simulation of robots which interact with the environment. He holds a European PhD from the Ecole des Mines de Paris with a thesis entitled "A framework for object recognition by active vision: using Bayes nets". The research work was carried out through a collaboration between INRIA (Institut National de Recherche en Informatique et en Automatique, Sophia Antipolis), project ICARE and the Robotics Research Group at Oxford University. He joined BT Laboratories in September 1997 to work in the Intelligent Systems Research Group. His main research interests include reasoning under uncertainty, learning, and studying the links between perception and action for intelligent systems.

 

Nader Azarmi

Nader Azarmi is the AI technology manager of Advanced Communications Engineering division and the technical manager of the Intelligent Systems Research group at BT Adastral Park. He joined BT in December 1989, working on the application of AI technology to telecommunications network management. He went on to set up an AI planning and scheduling R&D programme for BT's resource management. He is currently managing an international AI research and development programme covering scheduling, Agent and soft computing.

Nader has won the prestigious Institute for Operations Research and Management Sciences (INFORMS) medal-99, British Computer Society (BCS) IT medal-97, and BT Gordon Radley prize-98 for his technical work on scheduling technology and its novel applications in BT. He has also led his agent and soft computing projects to winning BCS IT award-97, BCS IT medal-98, and 5 BT technical awards.

Prior to joining BT, Nader spent many years of his academic life at Manchester, Essex, Kingston and London universities.

 

Behnam Azvine

Behnam Azvine holds a BSc in Mechanical Engineering, a MSc and a PhD in Control Systems from Manchester University. His previous work experience include: Research associate and assistant professor (lecturer) in the Dynamics and Control Research group of the Engineering department of Manchester University working on theoretical control, robust stability and smart system design, and Associate professor (senior lecturer) in the Control and Instrumentation research group of Staffordshire University working on smart structures and Control of Robots. He is currently leading a research team responsible for Soft Computing and Computational Intelligence techniques in the Intelligent Systems Research Group at BT laboratories. He regularly gives presentations in international conferences and workshops on the application of AI in Telecommunications, has more than 20 external publications and currently holds a visiting fellowship at the Department of Engineering Mathematics of Bristol University. He is also one of the chairmen of the European network of excellence for uncertainty management techniques. His current research interests include the application of Fuzzy Logic, Neural Networks and Evolutionary computing to human-centred computing, intelligent system design, model-based control and adaptive software systems.

 

Kwok Ching Tsui

Kwok Ching Tsui is a member of the intelligent systems research group. KC is a software engineer by training and has worked in an international bank for five years. His role in the bank included design and implementation of software systems as well as deploying new system development methodology. He joined BT in 1996 after finishing his PhD in Neural Computing. His current research interests include theory and application of soft computing, intelligent information agents and their application to call centres, and intelligent multimodal user interface.

 

Wayne Wobcke

Wayne Wobcke is a Senior Research Fellow in BT's Intelligent Systems Research Group at Adastral Park. After graduating with a PhD in Computer Science (1989) from the University of Essex, he taught for 9 years as a lecturer in the Basser Department of Computer Science at the University of Sydney, before joining BT. His main research is on the application of logic in Artificial Intelligence to formalise problems in knowledge representation, natural language processing and agent-based computing. His current work concerns the integration of soft computing and classical AI techniques, with application to diary management and to negotiation in multi-agent systems.
