Agent Based Modelling: Treating Customers as Individuals not Numbers

Modelling the Uptake of a Product

The diffusion of an innovation (knowledge or actual take up of a new product or service) is the process by which that innovation is communicated through channels over time among the members of a social system, [1]. The key aspects required to understand the diffusion process and the adoption of a new product are therefore the methods and nature of communications and the cognitive process involved in assessing the utility or profitability of that product. In conventional modelling of the diffusion process, there is no way to describe how the microscopic attributes of the members of the social system give rise to the observed rate, extent or order of the diffusion process. Agent based modelling provides an alternative approach which is successful at overcoming these short falls. We use an agent based model to investigate the effects of the structure of the communication system (the social network) and cognitive processes based on bandwagon theory on the nature of the diffusion process. These studies allow us to learn how to influence the take up of a new product or service in a real population of consumers, and hence give insights into developing new marketing strategies.

In constructing an agent based model of the diffusion of an innovation within a population of consumers, we need to create a conceptual model of consumers' cognitive behaviour and create an accurate and detailed picture of the social network within the population.

For the cognitive processing, there are a number of theories which shed light onto the pressures behind human consumption - these include theories on human need, social comparison theories, conditioning theories, decision and choice theories among others, [2]. For any modelling we need to simplify the processes but retain a suitable level of detail and realism. What appear to be consistent elements of the cognitive processes are an internally and an externally driven process. The internal process involves assessing the product's ability to satisfy the need of an individual without reference to outside influence. The externally driven process involves other individuals influencing the need or characteristics of an innovation, such as demonstrating that an individual is following a trend or gains more information on a product allowing the individual to make a reasoned decision on an adoption. In general there will be a combination of the two components for a particular innovation but with different weights depending on the specific innovation and individual in question.

The social networks are crucial elements which provide the channel for the information to drive the external process mentioned above. They allow the inclusion of important economic, psychological and sociological concepts and theories such as fads and social learning theories. Also, research has identified some important features of social networks, [3] which can have a profound influence on the extent and speed of an innovation diffusion. These are potentially crucial processes to understand so that we can identify structures and key types of individual within the population on whom influence can be exerted.
