Agent Based Modelling: Treating Customers as Individuals not Numbers

Authors

David Collings

David Collings was educated at Imperial College, University of London, and Cambridge University where he was awarded a first degree and a PhD in Physics respectively. David has worked in the area of computer engineering for GCHQ and in 1996 joined BT where he works in the Business Modelling Group, researching new tools and techniques for business modelling. David has published a number of papers in the fields of solid state physics and business modelling and is the author of a commercial computer software package marketed by a major Japanese radio amateur equipment manufacturer.

 

Iqbal Adjali

Iqbal Adjali joined BT in 1992 after receiving a doctorate in Theoretical Physics from Oxford University. Iqbal worked in the areas of distributed systems modelling, non-linear dynamics and fractal geometry and won the first prize of the Gordon Radley Awards in 1994. Since 1996, he has been working in the Business Modelling group where he is currently leading the Computational Economics team. He completed the BT MSc in telecom engineering in 1997 and has published in fields such as evolutionary economics, game theory, fractal modelling, distributed systems modelling and particle physics.

 

Peter Crocker

Peter Crocker is studying for a MEng degree at Oxford University. He worked in the Business Modelling Group at BT, helping implement and run agent based simulations, as part of his summer placement.

 

Michael Lyons

Michael Lyons has over 20 years experience of telecommunications research, which has included device development for optical communications systems, and studies in the areas of display technologies and the environmental impact of telecommunications.

Michael currently leads ACE's Business Modelling Group working on economic and business models of the future telecommunications industry. The group's work includes research into regulatory issues, industry structure and future service demand, as well as the development of computer simulation techniques of the Business Modelling Group. He has published a number of papers in the areas of business modelling and chemistry.

 

Tony Reeder

Tony Reader holds a BSc in Physics from Birmingham University and a PhD in Physics from Nottingham University. He was a post-doctoral research assistant at SUNY Buffalo in infra-red semiconductor spectroscopy. He joined BT in 1988 as a semiconductor analyst. While at BT he has been involved in the use of artificial intelligence for decision support systems. He also led a team researching new techniques for business modelling. Tony has recently moved to work on interconnect.
