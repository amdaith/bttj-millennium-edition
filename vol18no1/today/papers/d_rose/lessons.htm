Videoconferencing for Speech and Language Therapy in Schools

Lessons Learned Nursery - visual and audio environment

The high-quality videoconferencing systems used in the TeachSpeech project are utilised in both an application and an environment that is novel. This type of equipment is traditionally used for corporate videoconferencing applications to facilitate remote meetings between groups of people separated over large geographical distances, often internationally. Typically the equipment is located in dedicated videoconferencing suites and company board / meeting rooms. Usually these are carefully designed locations providing an optimised and physically secure environment to achieve the best possible visual and sound quality. These rooms have little or no background noise; the lighting is bright, balanced in intensity and well diffused. The visual background is plain, without background movement yet visually comfortable.

With regard to the physical placement of the trial videoconferencing terminals, space inside school buildings is generally at a premium and there is consequently very little, if any, unused space especially where a private and quiet location is sought. The school staff rooms were chosen as the locations and, through judicious scheduling of TeachSpeech appointments, session times are selected during the day when the staff rooms are not occupied for other reasons. An additional benefit of using staff rooms is that these are non-public rooms providing a more secure location for the equipment. Where possible the videoconferencing terminals, and hence camera view, are positioned to avoid sight of high-level light sources such as windows.

However, staff rooms are still challenging locations within which to use videoconferencing equipment and the scope for environmental alterations is limited. Inside school buildings there can be literally hundreds of sources of background noise, from children in adjacent classrooms, canteens, corridors between classrooms, playgrounds, or from individuals who need access through the same room or those that share that room, e.g. staff room. Microphone pick up of background noise has the unwanted effect of decreasing the sound clarity of the speech transmitted over the videoconferencing link, with the potential result of reducing the effectiveness of the interaction.

The nature of the TeachSpeech application and the subsequent demand placed on the technical quality of the sound channel is high. The initial subjective tests had indicated that, to ensure successful delivery of remote speech and language therapy support services, the sound channel needed to provide clarity in addition to intelligibility. Sound intelligibility, the ability to understand what is being said, is a basic requirement of all videoconferencing systems. However, the accompanying sound clarity, the clearness of the sound, varies depending upon the quality of the videoconferencing system. Audio clarity needs to be such as to enable subtle differences in speech pronunciation and construction to be clearly distinguishable. For example, the SLT might need to audibly demonstrate a particular speech exercise to the ESA that helps a child distinguish the pronunciation between "tap" and "cap" - this is a far more stringent and demanding requirement on sound quality than when videoconferencing systems are used within conventional business scenarios.

The microphones supplied as standard with the VC6000 videoconferencing terminals were quickly found to be unsuitable for the TeachSpeech application. These microphones are intended for use on the flat surface of a table, located centrally to the conference participants. They have been optimised to detect sound from within the whole room aided by the use of automatic gain control functions. In a noisy environment they are overly sensitive and pick up unwanted background noise, significantly reducing speech clarity.

As the interactions in the TeachSpeech project are primarily between two participants, the SLT and the ESA the standard microphone is not appropriate. An optimised microphone arrangement was devised that provided greater acoustic coupling between the participant and microphone, so minimising detection and transmission of unwanted background sounds. There are various microphone solutions available that are designed for specific sound qualities and levels of noise cancellation.

The microphone solution selected was a wired, miniature, professional quality tie-clip microphone. This was found to provide a compromise between ease of use (physical positioning) and sound quality, whilst minimising pickup of unwanted sounds. Although a wireless radio microphone would have been even easier to use, this was considered unsuitable for confidentiality reasons. Also it would have resulted in the introduction of an ongoing battery maintenance requirement. The tie-clip microphone used required a preamplifier to be connected to the audio input of the videoconferencing terminal. A professional quality preamplifier was selected that allowed internal modification to enable matching of the gain level to that required by the VC6000.

It was found that due to the location of the videoconferencing terminal in the I CAN Salisbury nursery, it was necessary to insert a 2m x 2m light blue coloured back drop panel centrally behind the seat of the SLT. This backdrop is required to prevent the remote participants, especially the children, being distracted by the background visual content, including colourful nursery wall pictures and toys.
