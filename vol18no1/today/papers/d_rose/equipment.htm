Videoconferencing for Speech and Language Therapy in Schools

Equipment Selection

Subjective tests were designed and carried out at BT at Adastral Park to determine the optimum acceptable technical specification of the videoconferencing technology. The subjects were two I CAN speech and language therapists, taking turns in role playing the expert SLT and remote recipient of the therapy during a typical teaching session.

The metrics chosen to be measured were:

  • comfort for SLT - the SLT may work all day at their terminal
  • comfort for child
  • school environment - the school will have to house, protect and maintain the components
  • image quality - could the participants see each other well enough to perform the therapy task?
  • sound quality - could the SLT and remote participant hear each other well enough to perform the therapy task?
  • lip synchronisation - did the viewed image correspond adequately with the sound output from the videoconferencing terminal?
  • screen size - what is the considered optimum useful screen size, and is it different for therapist and child?

Various configurations of physical equipment and ISDN bandwidth were tested. The SLT and recipient's systems were located in separate rooms. After each configuration was tried a questionnaire of subjective statements was graded on an analogue scale from 'strongly disagree' to 'strongly agree', and extra comments recorded. The nine subjective statements asked were:

Q1 I felt able to do my job using this equipment.

Q2 the conversation felt natural, using the equipment had little effect on me.

Q3 I felt I could do my job with this equipment as well as I could do my job face-to-face.

Q4 I would be in favour of using this equipment in my work.

Q5 I would prefer to use this equipment in my work than continue travelling to meet face to face.

Q6 the image I saw was of acceptable quality.

Q7 the sound I heard was of acceptable quality.

Q8 the movement of the image and sound were synchronised.

Q9 the test environment was a reasonable simulation of the intended environment for this equipment.

The SLT's subjective statements for each test were listed for reference, as were the recipient's statements that also made the same points but were worded slightly differently. The video and audio transmitted between the SLT and recipient was recorded on videotape, and used as an extra source of comments recorded on the questionnaire. Analysis of the experimental data identified an optimal technical specification for the remote provision of speech and language therapy - this was not difficult as many variables of the specification were quickly eliminated (e.g. 50 inch screens too expensive, 9 inch screens too small to portray detail). The basic technical specification of the equipment to be deployed in the TeachSpeech project trial is listed in table 1. This specification was selected for both the SLT and the recipient equipment.

Parameter: Technical Specification:
Link bandwidth 384 kbps (6B, three ISDN2 lines)
Visual compression CIF / H261
Audio bandwidth 7 KHz / 48 kbps
Screen size 51 cm acceptable
Lip synchronisation Sound and vision synchronisation provided by codec is acceptable

Table 1 Technical Specification of the Trial Videoconferencing Equipment

The subjects completed a final questionnaire to verify that an acceptable system had been identified, to be sure the optimal technical configuration was not merely 'the best of a bad bunch'. The following statements were graded 'strongly disagree / disagree / neutral / agree / strongly agree':

  • I believe I have seen a system I would be happy to use.
  • I believe videoconferencing can provide sufficient image quality for my use.
  • I believe videoconferencing can provide sufficient audio quality for my use.
  • The test environment was a reasonable simulation of the intended environment for this equipment.

Both subjects answered 'Strongly agree' to all these statements. Using the technical specification in table 1, the BT VC6000 videoconferencing terminal was identified as suitable for use by the TeachSpeech project. The VC6000, illustrated in figure 9 is a compact and fully mobile roll-about terminal.

 

Fig 9. VC6000 Video Terminal

It has a single 68 cm screen and is supplied with an integral pan/tilt/zoom camera that is controlled from a handheld remote control console.

Each participant is able to remotely control and position either their own or the remote end camera. The model of VC6000 selected contained the optional '3 x ISDN2' interface. Further tests were performed on a separate occasion using the VC6000 systems, to confirm that the audio, visual, and lip synchronisation qualities were acceptable in the actual environment of the trial. Five VC6000 terminals were selected for use by the project.
