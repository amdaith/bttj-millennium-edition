Videoconferencing for Speech and Language Therapy in Schools

Introduction

TeachSpeech [1] is a collaborative project between BT at Adastral Park and ICAN [16], which is researching the use of videoconferencing technology to support the delivery of speech and language therapy to children in mainstream schools. Compared with conventional telephone technologies, videoconferencing provides enhanced levels of telepresence, i.e. a greater feeling of being present at a remote location. This has been demonstrated in a number of telemedical and tele-education studies of remote delivery of expertise [2, 3, 4, 5, 6].

In this project, videoconferencing is used by a speech and language therapist (SLT) to communicate with children and their local assistants at remotely located schools. A key driver in the creation of the TeachSpeech project, and the use of videoconferencing in this application, is that adequate access to "professional advice for the teacher and the parents is essential in cases of speech problems" [7]. Intense frustration or distress caused by a failure to acquire language skills at school, such as reading and writing, can result in severe behavioural problems which in extreme cases may require psychiatric intervention [8]. It is estimated that 2.5 million people in the UK have a speech or language difficulty, including 5% of children entering schooling [9]. It is an aim of the current government to "...increase the level and quality of inclusion within mainstream schools…" for children with special educational needs [10]. In 1997 the NHS was able to provide on average of 8.2 SLTs per 100 000 of the population, yet research has indicated a need for 26.2 SLTs per 100 000 of the population [11]. Although videotelephony is becoming increasingly popular in UK schools and colleges for educational applications, videotelephony applications for speech and language impairment "is an area where almost no work has been done" yet people with language impairments "will most probably benefit from using videotelephony in their communication" [12]. The technology being deployed within TeachSpeech has the potential to make more effective use of the SLT services currently available to meet the needs of children in the UK.

Two key project aims are to demonstrate technical feasibility and to evaluate the effectiveness of using videoconferencing technology for the delivery of speech and language support services. Assessment of the effectiveness of the technology considers clinical effectiveness, quality, costs and time spent in use. It is the intention of this evaluation to provide an indication as to whether this novel concept could become both a commercially and clinically viable proposition.

Following a joint BT / I CAN application to the Department of Trade and Industry's (DTI's) Information Society Initiative Multimedia Demonstrator Programme [13], the project was awarded a grant covering 50% of the project costs, with I CAN meeting its remaining project costs through fundraising activities. TeachSpeech is a three year research project due to finish in 1999.
