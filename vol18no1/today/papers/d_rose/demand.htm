Videoconferencing for Speech and Language Therapy in Schools

Demand for Therapy Services

In the UK children with a significant speech and language impairment likely to impact on their education are typically assessed by a specialist and are identified as having a special educational need. UK schools maintain a register of children with special education needs and have a responsibility to organise the provision of the specialist services the child may require [14].

These needs are met by professional support which is conventionally delivered in a number of ways, depending upon the needs of the children and source of the support, including attendance by the specialist at the child's mainstream school or by the child attending a clinic, specialist language school or nursery.

In the United Kingdom, support to children in local authority schools is frequently provided by an 'Educational Support Assistant' (ESA). The ESA has the responsibility of delivering the child's day-to-day therapy programme. The programme is devised and developed in consultation with a professional SLT. A National Health Service (NHS) SLT would typically visit the school regularly, and leave written instructions to direct the ESA.

Due to limited resources, face-to-face consultations between the ESA and SLT may only take place once or twice per term, so limiting the monitoring of the child's progress and updating of the therapy programme to suit the changing needs of the child. The lack of SLT resource is exacerbated in rural areas where the SLT may spend significant time travelling to and from the school.

In Salisbury, Wiltshire, I CAN have a specialist speech and language therapy nursery for preschool-age children in a room at Woodlands County First School. Children attend this nursery on a part-time basis for one academic year prior to entering mainstream schooling. Should a child require further support, this is provided at the mainstream school as previously described, with the local NHS therapist visiting the child. The TeachSpeech project is investigating whether videoconferencing-based consultations can provide an effective means of delivering therapy support services once the child has entered mainstream schooling. The use of the video link in this way is considered appropriate, in this instance, as the I CAN SLT already has experience of working directly with the child and has prior knowledge of his or her personal communications difficulties.
