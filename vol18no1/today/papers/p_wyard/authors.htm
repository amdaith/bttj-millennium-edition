All Channels Open: Multimodal Human-Computer Interfaces

Authors

Peter Wyard

Peter Wyard is leader of the MUeSLI Multimodal spoken Language Interface project at Adastral Park, which is investigating the combination of advanced spoken dialogue with gestural input.

After graduating in 1976 Peter taught physics for ten years before joining the Language Group at BT. His early research was into novel methods of computational language learning. In conjunction with Charlie Nightingale, he developed a new neural net architecture called HODYNE, the Higher Order Dynamic Topology Net. He also researched the use of novel representations for learning context-free grammars using genetic algorithms. After a brief spell on speech translation, Peter moved into the speech technology area for a while, where his main job was project manager of the speech technology evaluation project. In 1993 he returned to the Language Group to work on spoken language systems. Since then he has led a number of projects in this area, producing the BusCat business catalogue system, the MailSec email access system, and Pik-a-pic, the movie database access system. Three years ago he migrated into multimodality, and has since been leading the MUeSLI project. His main research interests are language learning, robust language understanding, and multimodal system design.

From 1997-9 Peter was also business sector support manager for Global Industry, which provided welcome opportunities to relate BT's technology to our customer needs in a very direct fashion.

 

Gavin Churcher

Gavin Churcher has been working with the Language Technology Group since joining BT in November 1997. He is currently working on the Multimodal Spoken Language Interfaces project with Peter Wyard. After graduating in 1994 from Leeds University, he remained to complete a PhD which examined the use of linguistic knowledge, particularly that of dialogue, in the performance of speech recognition applications. His current research interests follow on from this and include robust multimodal dialogue interfaces, virtual worlds and synthetic personae.
