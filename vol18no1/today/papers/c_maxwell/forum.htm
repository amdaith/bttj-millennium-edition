The Future of Work: Understanding the Role of Technology

The Forum

The Forum is an on line collaborative working environment aiming to bring people together both informally and formally. It is designed to allow people who should meet each other to do so easily and naturally, and provides the means for them to have richer online meetings.

The Forum can be divided into two parts. The Forum Contact Space and the Forum Meeting Space. The Forum Contact Space is an environment where workers who are not physically together can maintain an awareness of their colleagues. It makes use of normal desktop activity to help visualise and share those informal communication cues that are lost when we are physically dispersed.

The Forum Meeting Space is focused on synchronous meetings and is based around audio conferencing. The Meeting Space is a graphically mediated audio-conferencing tool, which is integrated with the Contact Space and uses the same server architecture and platform.

Both spaces exploit people's existing skills in negotiating communications, which they already use in a physical space. This minimises the cognitive overhead for the users and allows them to concentrate on the objectives rather than the mechanics of the interaction.

The design is derived from observations of what really happens in shared offices: chance meetings, recruited conversations, seeing what people are doing and when they are available. This unplanned, unforced, social interaction is a fundamental aspect of sharing a real space with people and enables them to build up a sound relationships grounded in mutual understanding and trust.

The Forum makes use of a system called Symbolic Acting. When the users carries out their normal activity on the desktop machine, the avatar, representing them in the virtual space, acts out the symbolic meaning of this activity. For instance, in the Forum Meeting Space, when a user opens the whiteboard application, their avatar is seen to stand up in front of a whiteboard. In this way everyone knows who is using the whiteboard, without the user having to actively control both their desktop and their avatar.

 

Movie 3. The use of a Whiteboard

Similarly, in the Contact Space, when the user is writing a document on a particular subject, for instance, JavaScript, the Contact Space Agent puts their avatar in the 'JavaScript' interest area in the Contact Space on the desktop. The user is then in 'visual proximity' with other people in the Contact Space system, who are working in a similar area. The user is then free to speak to, or politely ignore others. This process will put people together based on their subject interests or activity, such as copying files. This echoes the 'Meeting by the Photocopier' effect.
 

Movie 4. Avatars meeting in the Contact Space

The Forum is one of the technical solutions, which will facilitate a new future for the way that people work. It will allow people to collaborate more effectively. It will encourage a greater awareness of the presence of immediate team members and other colleagues who are less closely linked to their every day work, regardless of their physical location. The Contact Space is potentially, a particularly big step forward, in that it emulates many of the important but subtle factors about working in the same space together: making new contacts, maintaining a network of people working on similar activities. It means that physically separated individuals do not need to feel so isolated.

Early results from trials

In the very early stages of developing such innovative product and services ideas, potential users have no rules for how they might use novel communications tools, so the best approach is to try them out and see how the users react.

The Forum project is committed to a strong user-centred design process. Where possible, external user feedback is integrated into continuing development. Coupled with this we are committed to using the products we develop ourselves. Evaluation and trials are balanced between this external and internal sampling. A range of methods is used in such trials. In the initial stages of development, focus groups are useful for discussing initial reactions to new design concepts. This approach has been used with the Contact Space. Observation, questionnaires and interviews with users of working prototypes are also appropriate at later stages. Observation of members of the design team holding meetings in an initial prototype of the Meeting Space have also been carried out, followed by interviews [13].

The focus groups were held with different sets of workers: Students, Knowledge workers and Flexible workers. These groups had very different opinions and concerns regarding the concept and the design of the Contact Space. The age of the participants seems to be a significant factor, affecting their reactions to the Contact Space. The younger participants seemed more experienced in Internet communication media such as mailing lists, newsgroups, chatrooms and buddy lists (ICQ/PAL). As a group, the students were more at ease with the concept of a work-orientated virtual social area. Older respondents were more reluctant to vary or add to their favoured (and established) communication media of e-mail, telephone and face-to-face contact. The younger respondents were also less concerned about any overheads involved with using the system, for example, setting one's state or avatar to 'busy', and were more confident that desktop activities could be successfully used to represent some sort of contextual accessibility information.

There was also a notable split between job types, as the need for communication and the role of communication varies depending on what sort of job a person does. For instance, people whose work was quite independent and could rely on documented support felt that the communication channels offered in the Contact Space were too different to face-to-face contact in the way communication is initiated and negotiated. Those people who felt a need to discuss ideas or informally review work perceived a potential benefit in having new channels available to approach sets of people.

The internal field trial of the Meeting Space with members of the design team was carried out with an initial prototype. This has proved very beneficial even though the prototype had a limited range of avatar designs. The sample in the trial was biased but the trial still gave valuable insights into mediated meetings and into the functionality of the Meeting Space. Results showed that having a visual image of people in an audio conference was beneficial to the communicative confidence of users. This was particularly noticeable on entry to the conference, as new arrivals were often greeted before they spoke. Avatars would automatically wave to greet a new entrant, which was surprisingly popular - one user commented: "it sets a nice tone".

Automated avatar movements (symbolic acting) proved useful as triggers to initiate conversation. In some instances, the movement of an avatar would trigger a person to bring a colleague into the discussion. This situation is clearly a useful augmentation to audio conferencing.

The Meeting Space trial showed that data sharing capabilities provide a very strong case for the benefits of desktop conferencing, with reported advantages over face-to-face meetings. The Meeting Space showed good potential for adding a needed social element to audio conferencing.

Trust Issues associated with the Forum

The trials have enabled us to begin exploring users' feelings about of a number of issues, which will be important factors in the future world of work. These were originally highlighted in previous BT trials with flexible workers, whose working practices give a useful early indicator of likely areas of concern. The issues of particular relevance to the Forum centre on the dynamics of trust and control in the workplace. In terms of the ABC Trust model, the Forum is part of the situational context of any interaction. As the design of the Forum evolves it is important to improve our understanding of how users' attitudes to the issues of trust and control help define their emergent behaviour within the Forum.

Trust is an important part of the team relationships, which underpin their 'will to produce'. The introduction of technologies such as the Forum will affect the trust relationship between users. Most flexible working is currently done with people who already know their work colleagues. Such knowledge of individuals and shared experience is a critical basis for trust in a relationship. This is normally best established through frequent, face to face interaction. The Forum Contact Space and Meeting Space may make it easier for individuals, who have very little previous knowledge of each other, to build up an effective working relationship. The way that users are represented in the Forum can help them through the process of establishing a rapport at the beginning of any specific interaction. This process of establishing mutual orientation is a key building block in developing trust. The importance of this early stage in a meeting was reflected by the popularity in the Meeting Space trial of the greetings phase of the meeting. The issue of trust is even more significant in the Contact Space since the situation is, by definition, much less clearly defined. The user has less knowledge about who is there and why, than when attending a meeting. As a result users are not able to establish mutual orientation so easily at the beginning of an interaction. The system should therefore aim to engender trust where it is justified and allow people to understand when it is not justified. As users gain experience of using the Forum, the consequences of previous interactions will affect their attitudes (i.e. the antecedents) in future interactions.

The Forum, like any innovative technology, also raises a number of issues related to the balance of power within a work group. As the Contact Space opens up new possibilities for people to make contact with others, a potential barrier to acceptance could be that some individuals perceive that they may lose the power and influence, which they gain from being a gatekeeper or an expert. A common problem associated with the introduction of new technology is that its introduction means that those users who are able to make the most effective use of the new tools have an immediate advantage over others. The significance of symbolic acting in this respect is that it gives minimal advantages to the user who is able to manipulate the system most effectively in any interaction, because the Forum does some of this for the user.

It is also important to encourage interaction between managers and their staff. Physical separation of the manager from their staff can undermine this relationship as it makes communication more difficult. The Contact Space also offers the opportunity, for instance, of including a built-in 'coaching/managing' aspect, which could encourage informal interaction between managed and manager at relevant moments. There is a risk that management could also abuse such a system and if users are suspicious of the system as a 'big brother' then it will not work. The approach to this problem within the Forum is to put the emphasis on movement and proximity and on the cues for negotiating communication, rather than monitoring and displaying explicit detail. The Forum also allows the users to see the view that others will see of them. The Contact Space will allow the users to look busy on specific occasions when they need to but will still hold them to account over a long term, thus addressing the conflicting interests of the manager and the managed.

Confidence about feeling in control of the system is related to the degree of trust the individuals have in the technology. This was reflected in the attitudes of the focus groups towards the Contact Space. Several people expressed a concern about being able to flag themselves as unavailable. The desire for individuals to restrict their accessibility highlights a potential conflict between the short-term need of the individual to make progress with their work and the long-term need to encourage interaction within the group. This interaction has two particular benefits: it helps integrate the individual within the group and the exchange of information and ideas encourages creativity and innovation within the work group. Isolation is a commonly reported problem for teleworkers and will probably continue to be an issue for individuals in the future, as working lives become more flexible and complex. Conveying a feeling of belonging is particularly difficult via computer mediated communications. The Contact Space is trying to directly address this issue in its design. Introducing people to others with similar interests, who may be beyond their immediate work group, is also an important aspect of the Contact Space. As people's work activities and interests change, the Contact Space could potentially be used to improve the connectedness of the organisation as a whole.

If these issues can be successfully resolved in systems such as the Forum this will open up new possibilities for effective collaboration between individuals who work together but are physically dispersed.
