The Future of Work: Understanding the Role of Technology

Understanding Trust

The world of work is rapidly becoming more uncertain, dynamic and complex. Driven by the increasing globalisation and competitiveness of the economic environment, organisations and their individual members must constantly seek to exploit the opportunities offered by new technology. Improved communications links support the flexibility of working at any time in any place but this is only effective when managers do not feel the necessity for direct supervision of the workforce. One of the key elements underpinning relationships at work is trust. This issue has been the subject of intensive research in recent years [10, 11].

Research being carried out at BT aims to understand the dynamic complexity of an organisation and the relationships of the individuals within it. This makes it possible to design technical solutions, which overcome not only the physical barrier of distance but also reinforce the strength of the relationships within a group of co-workers. The theoretical model of trust, presented here, has been developed as a tool to help understand the working environment.

A model of trust in organisations

Based on the analysis of case studies of virtual businesses a trust model has been constructed to show what factors affect the level of trust in any interactions between organisations or individuals and how they relate together. This is known as the ABC model of trust (Antecedents, Behaviour and Consequences) [12].


Fig 1 The ABC model of trust

The model contains four groups of variables:

  • Antecedents of trust: These establish the context.
  • Behavioural elements: These lead directly to the amount of trust shown in actual behaviour.
  • Consequences which result from this behaviour.
  • Monitoring of performance then leads to modification of the antecedents of trust before any future interaction.

The variables within these groups and their interdependencies are now explained in more detail:

Antecedents of Trust

Two variables are classed as antecedents of trust:

  • Innate trust;
  • Situational context

Innate (or dispositional) trust is the natural tendency of an individual to trust others, and is considered to be a function of their attitudes, personality, culture and previous experience. It is independent of a specific situation and is a measure of an individual's tendency to trust or distrust.

The situational context includes a number of factors such as:

  • the nature of the task being considered,
  • previous experience the members of the work group have of each other,
  • the extent of their shared background,
  • skills and abilities of the participants,
  • the existing organisational processes which support trust.
Behavioural elements

The three key variables which influence a person's behaviour are:

  • the level of subjective trust (influenced by the antecedents of trust),
  • the perceived risk involved,
  • the importance of the outcome.
Consequences

The consequences of the situation are the actual outcome of the decision on how to behave during any interaction.

Monitoring of Performance

The level of trust in a relationship is dynamic. The knowledge of one's partner gained from experience of their actual behaviour influences the level of subjective trust of all concerned. The consequences of the situation are monitored and any new knowledge about behaviour, attitudes or competence modifies the antecedents. In the short term, the biggest impact is on the situational context. In the long term, innate trust is also affected.

Application of the model

This model is now being used to analyse specific work situations where computer mediated communications systems can be used. By making the process of building trust explicit, it highlights how designs can be modified in order to maintain and enhance trust within a work group. Increasingly in the future, working relationships between members of a dispersed work group may not be built on face to face contact. Enabling people to develop trust within this context is critical for ensuring the effectiveness and acceptability of new communications technology.
