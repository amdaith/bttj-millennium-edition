Responsibilities

Why consider them?

I have analysed what goes on in the business and I know what the support systems must do. I have identified a process that can be automated and with a new system in place the business will reap rewards, both in financial terms and by improved quality of service. So why do I need to consider Roles and Responsibilities?

The answer is that things change. Much effort is expended on attempting to understand what things will change and how.

We find it relatively easy to anticipate that technology will take leaps forward and that this in turn will influence customer expectations. If you have a 4-year-old PC you are probably thinking of changing it.

Fig 1. Driving Expectation

So when a new element of OSS is designed, it will have some flexibility built in to allow for change. Perhaps the customer data can be extended to hold an e-mail address, or the Case-Based reasoning system can learn from new fault symptoms.

But the enterprise changes that are happening at the same time are not trying to solve these issues; and yet they influence the capabilities we require from OSS. An enterprise board of directors would not suggest organising the company into teams of those with mobile phones and teams without!

The flexibility we need to build into new support systems, is the flexibility to react to the enterprise organisation, i.e. 'who is responsible for what'. Indeed, as we find a market-place with short-lived services, providing solutions for the moment, the roles within the Customer enterprise become just as important as the roles within the Service Provider enterprise.

Let us take the example of a customer information database: The designers have been careful to build in flexibility, and the mail address can be added to the information held. This satisfies the functions and processes that were in the original model. A Role model, however, would expose the limitations of the system:

  • Who has responsibility to define the list of data items associated with a customer?

Traditionally, a system administrator would update some steering data controlling the template for the interaction, that the customer-handling team would ask for and note the new data item.

We cannot guarantee, though, that this will always be the case. A service might only be viable if the customer maintains their own 'customer data'. This is already the case with many Web Hosting Services. So clearly the assignment of responsibilities and the associated roles that discharge these responsibilities are key to identifying the requirements of support systems.

Within Telecommunications

An exhaustive list of the Roles that exist and responsibilities that are discharged during the supply of telecommunications services is well beyond the scope of this paper; indeed it is doubtful whether such a list exists. This should not, however, deter us from exploration, and an extract of the list will give us some insight into the implications on OSS.

'OSS at the edges' inspires us to explore the assignment of responsibilities, traditionally in the service provider domain, to the customer. The following table provides a list of services using communication networks and suggests some of the responsibilities that could be assigned to the customer. You can decide for yourself what implication this might have on the OSS.

Responsibility When this could be with the customer
Maintain Customer Details
  • A Web hosting service. Data is entered on a web-based form.
Service Instance Initialisation
  • A Web based search
  • A temporary virtual path in a corporate virtual LAN.
Problem Diagnosis after failure
  • A Web based search. eg: name entered incorrectly.
  • A virtual path in a LAN. eg: request exceeds paid-for capacity.
Bill Production
  • Credit card purchase over WWW. Customer checks total using tool on service provider's site.
