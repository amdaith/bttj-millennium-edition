Activity Characteristics

In 1999 BT led a Eurescom project [8] exploring what Operational Support will be in the future. Part of this project analysed the nature of the activities associated with operational support to see how these would change.

Fig 8. Operational Support Activities

Figure 8 above defines the Operational Support activities used. All aspects of supporting a business have been considered, from managing the business (BM) and purchasing the resources for it (SR) to Sales and provisioning instances of service (S&OP and SIP), ensuring quality of those services (QA and SRM) and collecting the correct levy for use (AcC and CEC).

Fig 9. Industry Role Model

A number of roles in the Information industry were defined and used in the study as shown in the figure 9. The Access Connector (AC) and the Network Connector (NC) are infrastructure roles providing two service-providing roles with 'connection' services. The two service providers are the Network Service Provider (nSP) (such as Intelligent Network style services e.g. freephone), and the higher value Service Provider (hvSP) who may be offering specific content of application level services to the market (e.g. a health service multimedia conferencing service).

The different roles that will be supported in the future Information Industry will have different interactions. They will be part of different supply chains and will have different positions in those supply chains. The nature of the Operational Support a player adopting them will have will differ significantly.

The role model adopted in this project tries to reflect the assumed information industry development by 2005, where eCommerce is ubiquitous between businesses (including the supplier role), where specialisation of network provisioning (access and core) is expected and where highly specialised service providers will dominate the service market.

Ten factors were used to describe and characterise an Operational Support activity:

  • the scale of the activity,
  • the degree of automation,
  • how routine it is,
  • how time-critical it is,
  • how internalised it is,
  • how interactive with other operational activities it is,
  • how reactive or proactive it is,
  • how visible it is to the customer,
  • the value to the enterprise,
  • the value that others placed on it.
