Customer Contact

The Nature of Contact

Today customer contact takes on many different forms, with the more traditional forms of contact such as postal, personal visits and telephone calls remaining prevalent. However, technological advances in communications means that customer contact is increasingly taking on the electronic form. For example, via BT's Web site customers are able to view and edit their friends and family numbers, also to visit the BT shops and purchase goods and services. Such customer contact requires interactive applications, and full integration to support systems, rather than just offering the customer simple browsing facilities. Imagine the annoyance to customers if they were to change their friends and family numbers via the Internet, only for the support systems to fail to recognise these changes.

The expansion in the use of the Internet and electronic contact by customers will necessitate changes in the way all service providers handle customer contact. The self-service approach might suggest that customers are likely to receive more standard automated responses. Such automated responses are not personalised, and certainly lack any kind of 'human touch'. Granted that some customers may be happy with the automated contact, but some will doubtless eventually begin to feel automated themselves ('just another customer number'). So when customers do make a more traditional form of contact (possibly due to a fault or query), and actually speak to a member of a customer service team it is important they are dealt with in a professional yet empathic nature. Hence the maximum 'value' is both assigned to, and extracted from, customer contact situations.

Support systems should in no way distract from the personal touch and make the customer feel they are just being processed, or dealing with an automaton. Systems that accurately record customer data (which also includes updating any changes in customer data), and enable timely solution of problems, fault fixing and billing enquiries are necessary. The same systems should also enable the contact agent to offer the customer accurate and timely advice on the products and services that are right for them. For example, many SME companies directly compete with large corporates, but they often lack large IT budgets and departments. Therefore they need integrated propositions tailored to their individual needs. Any 'solutions company' has the responsibility to provide such customers with not only the right information but also to pitch the level of dialogue/interaction to their level of knowledge. Competition will require companies to provide extended aftercare services, and avoid adopting a sell-and-forget mentality.

Bt's world-class service is going to be put under more pressure as global relations begin to grow; we really need to be the best in the world. It is vital that our people receive not only world-class training in customer handling but also world-class support systems they can rely on.

We must make customers feel not only that they have been treated professionally, but also that they are more than a number and they have an identity. Customers should receive a strong and positive feel good factor about and from the company - that's our world-class service. This personal touch is the means to ensuring customer loyalty.

Conversational analysis

So how does customer contact map out with regard to roles and responsibilities in providing customers with their needs today? Below is an attempt to explore and analyse conversations between residential customers and BT. The beauty of looking at conversational analysis [6] is that it is not dependent on whether the interaction has occurred electronically or verbally.

  Customer (residential) BT
Mutuality low high
Significance high low
Control high/ medium low
Informedness high (to low?) medium/high

Mutuality

BT has a high responsibility to ensure that the product and service sold meets the customer's requirements, and the correct support systems are operational. All the customer has responsibility for is to pay the bill. However, the low-high mutuality between the customer and BT may not be the case if BT still had the pre-regulation monopoly on telecommunications. The increasing levels of competition in the market-place and resulting consumer choice has forced service provider companies, like BT, to become more customer-focused.

In the future, the high value of mutuality will continue to grow as pressure on BT to meet its customer requirements increases.

Significance

BT deals with many residential customers every day, therefore the significance of the conversation between the customer and BT is high-low. However, if the customer were a multi-national corporate, then the significance may be switched to high-high, as a large corporate contract has greater significance to BT because of their higher financial gains and reputation value compared to an individual residential customer.

Possibly the distinction companies make between residential customers and larger corporates is a hindrance to quality of service, if the systems and processes used are the same. While it is unrealistic to suggest every residential customer should have the same significance as a corporate customer, it is important to remember that a residential customer may be the chairman of the large multinational corporate. Also some residential customers may be home workers, and may be part of a large corporate company.

Control

Most or all of the control is with the customer when purchasing products or services. The customer initiates the proceedings, and can withdraw from the proceedings at any time. Therefore the control the customer has on proceedings is considered high (however as some of Bt's products and services are exclusive to BT, this control may be medium-high). Though, if the seller had a complete monopoly then the customer control is low because they do not have the option to take their custom elsewhere.

Informedness

The customer knows what their requirements are, and companies like BT know about their products and services; therefore the informedness is high on both sides.

However, if dealing with a customer with little IT knowledge, the informedness may be low on the customer side. The customer, for example, may know what they what to achieve but not the products and services they require. They are relying on BT to tell them what exactly are the best products and solutions for them as individuals. Therefore it is our commitment to offer them the right products and services, within their budgets, for their needs. We really have the chance to excel as a customer caring business when the customer informedness is low. Their reliance offers BT a means to provide extra value to them with something more than just a technical solution, but a sense of commitment and care.

Discussion

While conversation analysis does not magically come up with all the right answers, it helps to highlight potential problems which otherwise may be overlooked.

  • High customer control - BT is in a competitive industry with new market entrants appearing almost daily and competing against prices that are ever falling. As a result customers flit from one supplier to another with increasing ease and regularity and the only thing to which a customer is likely to remain loyal is the price. All this adds up to ever increasing amounts of customer control over the choice of their communications supplier.

  • Low customer significance - yet it is these same customers that have high control over who they chose to supply their communications needs that also have low significance to a large corporate such as BT.

  • Low customer mutuality - increasing levels of mutuality will also play an important role as customer satisfaction becomes paramount; the more choice they have, the more they need to be satisfied with the products and services they are sold.

It seems a dangerous combination for a residential customer to have high control and low significance. Is there anything BT and other communications companies can do about the high control? In short no, as there is no gain to be made in breaking anti-competitive regulatory rules and losing consumer confidence with lock in contracts. It is a symptom of the current market. However, the development of products, services and customer care which are unique and superior to other competitors may cause the customer to remain loyal to a company, rather than just loyal to the lowest price.

So what about the low significance that residential customers have? We could simply treat each and every customer with the same respect we would a higher paying corporate company. However, it is difficult for any company not to judge a customer by the money they generate. Perhaps the first step companies need to take is to get over this obsession with costs. For example, call automation may reduce call-waiting times and save a company money, but it is more likely to add to the problem of high customer turnover. Nothing can be more frustrating and less customer friendly than a system which does not allow a customer to actually speak to another human. (It is in our nature to want to do this and some things you just can not change.) This simple ability to talk to a human rather than a computerised recording may help add to the customer's feeling of significance to the company.

While BT and other service providers should be avoiding call automation, electronic forms of customer contact through e-mail and the web are on the increase. A lot has been said about the lack of trust people have in eCommerce. Similar trust problems may increasingly occur in eCustomer contact, and a well-known name will help to promote this trust. BT has an advantage over many new market entrants in that BT is a trusted, well-recognised brand name, with a wealth of experience in the communications industry. But this does not mean we have taken away our means of communications on a one-to-one basis with the customers. We should always continue to offer the customers a choice, whether they wish to talk or simply e-mail us. Somehow we have to form a bond with our customers, which means they are more then just loyal to the lowest price, but loyal to the company.
