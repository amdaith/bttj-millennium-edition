FreeUnet, Disruptive technology IP network research platform

Open Platforms

The routers in FreeUnet are built using standard PC hardware and make extensive use of Open Source software. The data transfer rate of typical PCI buses [1] (132 Mbyte/s, or 1,056 Mbps) used by modern PC motherboards will sustain throughput of network traffic at around 400 Mbps. Experimenters have reported such throughputs from PCI bus systems, and FreeUnet's routers achieve sustained throughputs around these levels using 100 BaseT Ethernet, ATM 155 Mb and recently 1000 BaseT (GigEthernet) PCI cards. PCI bus is also favoured for its price / performance ratio.

A large part of the research and development on IP networking and IETF (Internet Engineering Task Force) [2] standards around the world now takes place on Open Source operating systems such as FreeBSD [3] or Linux [4]. A good part of this work becomes available over the Internet under Open Source licences [5], such as the GNU General Public License [6] or the BSD License [7]. Therefore FreeUnet can deploy and test third party implementations of protocols and applications as soon as they are available, usually long before they are available in commercial turnkey routers. FreeUnet routers use the FreeBSD operating system.

Open Source software has come of age. It has shrugged off prejudice about its reliability and has long since gained acceptance with the technical community - in September 1999 55% of a surveyed 7.3 million Websites [8] were using the Open Source web server 'Apache Server' [9]. The business case for Open Source software is gaining acceptance, and Open Source promises to be one of the next truly disruptive technologies.

The network hosts used for development, testing and normal OA are Windows 95, 98 and NT PCs, PCs running FreeBSD and LINUX, Sun workstations and Hewlett Packard workstations.
