Nature-inspired Computation: Towards Novel and Radical Computing

Future Possibilities

In the previous sections we have shown how nature-inspired computing can offer different and effective solutions to real-world problems. These disparate techniques are all linked by their use of metaphors inspired by the natural world, but these are a small sample of the diversity of the natural world that could potentially be applied in this way. What does the future hold for this area?

First, it needs to be stated that there are still some major limitations that restrict the application of these techniques to really large-scale problems. These are the problems of scalability - of maintaining the same level of performance on the large scale as on the small - and robustness - maintaining performance when perturbed, especially by mutation. The applications shown here have begun to address these problems, but there is more to do.

We must learn more about evolvability - the capacity of a system to continually and indefinitely adapt to its environment over an evolutionary time-scale. What are the features of an evolutionary system that make it evolvable? Clearly biological systems have these in great abundance. Understanding evolvability requires drawing upon many different strands of research in computing and biology [22]. The process of development from genotype to phenotype, the redundancy of various genetic encodings, and the influence of rich and dynamic environments are all areas of research likely to have a big impact on the evolutionary computation field over the next two years.

New developments in flexible and adaptive techniques are important because of the rapid changes that are occurring in telecommunications today. On the one hand, there is increasing convergence of the telecommunications and computing sectors, both in the use of telecommunications and computing end-user equipment to access services previously solely in the other domain, and in the increasing importance of data transmission for telecommunications network providers. On the other, there is convergence between fixed and mobile telecommunications. Telecommunications in the future will need to provide more flexible, adaptable and decentralised services in order to respond to customer needs.

We have shown some of today's successful nature-inspired techniques, but they are by no means the last word in nature-inspired solutions. We expect more and different techniques to emerge in the future.
