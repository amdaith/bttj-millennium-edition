Living With Agents

Abstract

Information has never been more accessible, or more plentiful. But as the information age dawns a world is revealed where information is perhaps too plentiful, and yet still not quite accessible enough.

"Where is that file?"

"How can I contact my colleague?"

"What does this phrase mean?"

"How do I use this system?"

Anyone whose job involves manipulating information will recognise the problems. Fortunately we have search engines, knowledge bases, desktop assistants, and soon Agents. You may have already interacted with a software agent, like the 'wizards' that provide a smarter, easier to use interface in the latest software packages. There are also less obvious agents who work behind the scenes as intermediaries and translators, enabling previously insular systems to communicate with one another, forming a virtual society whose collective potential is much greater.

Here we consider how the agent technology being developed today will affect the life of a typical knowledge worker. We consider how an agent produces a personalised electronic 'Morning paper', and how with a report deadline imminent an agent assistant that is capable of finding, processing and presenting information, just in time.

Intriguingly, agents' abilities to find, interrogate, understand and negotiate mean that the biggest impact is likely to be outside the office - in electronic commerce. There are already agents in existence that can perform comparison shopping on behalf of their users. Whilst the near future promises agents that are experts on the purchase of certain items, and personalised to their owners' preferences.

Living with agents? You may not want to live without them.
