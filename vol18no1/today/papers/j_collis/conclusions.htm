Living With Agents

Conclusion

Software agent technology is based on over three decades of artificial intelligence research. These technologies are now approaching a level of maturity where they can be applied to manage the inherent complexity of the information age - and some might say, just in time. Consider the problem of 'information overload'. Personal agents which can analyse, filter and present information offer an enticing vision where only the most relevant information is delivered, and in a timely and appropriate manner dependent on the reader and their context.

There is great potential for agents who interact, and form virtual societies where information, expertise and services can be traded and exchanged. The Internet already provides individuals with direct access to a wealth of information and services, and the potential of a massive global market-place. Unfortunately finding and integrating these online resources is currently very difficult; what users want is a personal shopping agent - an intelligent, reliable proxy that is aware of its owner's personal preferences and constraints, and who can be reliably delegated tedious tasks like finding the best deals.

So, perhaps the future will see the emergence of personal agents that accompany their users throughout their life. These agents serve two roles, being information repositories that accumulate and organise the data generated by their user's day-to-day activities, as well as acting as their user's proxy, interacting with other agent services like those described. The technology is beginning to fall into place ... so don't be too surprised if in a couple of years you find yourself giving your Christmas shopping list to your personal shopping agent...
