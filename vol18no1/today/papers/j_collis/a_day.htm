Living With Agents

A Day in the company of Agents

Bob is having breakfast and reading the morning newspaper. This newspaper is one of a kind - The Daily Bob! Earlier this morning Bob's personal agent contacted several online news services and selected the stories that Bob's personal profile suggested would be of most interest. Bob has been using his agent for months, commending and criticising selections, as a result his agent has been adapting its profile, and now delivers stories that Bob always finds interesting and/or relevant.

Fig 2. A demonstration of 'The Bugle', a personalised News Agent.

Bob has reached the office and started work on a report on the potential for software agent services. Running on Bob's computer is an agent called Radar, which is monitoring the document Bob is composing. Midway through the first paragraph Bob pauses to think about an appropriate reference or example to illustrate the point he intends making. Radar takes advantage of this pause to send the current sentence to several databases, including an archive of Bob's email messages and technical summaries of the company's electronic library. The matching documents are ranked by their perceived relevance and titles displayed on Bob's screen in a small unobtrusive window. One match for a technical paper catches Bob's eye, so he clicks on the hyperlink to retrieve a summary, and a few potential ideas for his report.

Fig 3. A demonstration of 'Radar', the just-in-time information delivery agent.

One section of Bob's report concerns the use of agents for knowledge management; but this is not an area that Bob knows much about. An expert might be able to answer some of Bob's outstanding questions, but how can he find them? Fortunately most of the company's technical staff have registered their interests and expertise with an agent called Grapevine. So Bob asks Grapevine to suggest the best people to talk to about 'knowledge management', the agent matches the search term into its expertise categories and returns a list of relevant experts, their degree of expertise and contact details.

Fig 4. A demonstration of 'Grapevine', the expert-finding matchmaker agent.

Most of the information Bob needs for his report is available online, assuming of course that he can find it. It is ironic that the vast amount of material on the Internet is both its greatest attraction and its biggest weakness. Fortunately, there are now many agent systems many agent systems that can search for information intelligently, querying multiple search engines, using synonyms and their own searching expertise where appropriate, and filtering and ranking the results so that they are manageable.

It is now mid-evening and Bob is back at home. The advent of digital broadcasting has provided Bob with plenty of choice when it comes to entertainment, with hundreds of music, movie and television channels available. So Bob asks his personal agent to suggest some programming that matches his personal preferences. In response the agent begins 'collaborative filtering' of the entertainment schedules, using not only Bob's interests but also those of his close friends, which it obtains by interacting with their agents. This allows the agent to broaden its entertainment suggestions beyond what Bob has already seen and heard.

Fig 5. A demonstration of 'Pandora', the entertainment suggestion maker agent.

Tonight Bob also wants to book a short holiday abroad using his personal travel agent (PTA). So, he enters his preferences and asks his agent to find the best buys for flights, hotel accommodation and car rental. For the PTA to achieve this task with minimal intervention from its owner requires a considerable degree of intelligence. For instance, it will have to reason about what information is required, find it from the online travel information sources, and then understand and integrate the information into its current plan. The PTA will interact with databases like airline reservation systems, as well as other agents, like hotel room brokers, before compiling an itinerary that is presented for Bob's perusal. A few revisions later and Bob has an itinerary and price he is happy with, and authorises the PTA to confirm the booking.

Fig 6. A demonstration of the ZEUS PTA - the Personal Travel Agent.

With the trip planned, the PTA will monitor known online information sources for factors that might affect it. This information will be shared with Bob's personal News Agent, so that in the days prior to the holiday his personal morning newspaper will include pertinent stories about his destination, like the current weather conditions and events that will coincide with his stay. Even when Bob has left his house for the airport the PTA could monitor for potential flight delays and motorway traffic congestion and telephone them to Bob's hands-free mobile phone.
