New Products for Old: Making a Profit

Surfing the Data Wave

One of the major issues in the telecommunications industry at present is the move from a traditional business based almost entirely on telephony, towards a business which incorporates a high proportion of data and multimedia services. The change in the business paradigm demands an enormous investment in suitable transport mechanisms and capacity, to ensure that demand does not outstrip resources. This is being achieved by migrating from legacy networks to IP-based technology.

As part of this move, a model has been developed and used to provide guidance on the interconnect architecture between the traditional core telephone network and the IP domain, in the shape of the BeTaNet, an integrated multiservice broadband platform capable of carrying the Company's voice, data and video applications.

The aim of the modelling work was to identify the most appropriate architecture for offloading voice band data from the telephone network on to the IP network. As with other future-looking studies, there was considerable uncertainty as to demand levels, and it was imperative to build a 'what-if' capability into the model, to assess the impact of a variety of possible scenarios. The voice network segment of the service was dimensioned to the expected loading, and costs apportioned to yield a unit cost. The data-based part of the service was also dimensioned and costed, together with the interface equipment in each of the points of presence. An end-to-end unit cost per Megabit of data traffic could then be calculated for each interconnect option. The optimum architecture depends on the level of demand. The model has enabled guidelines to be mapped out on the evolution of the architecture, as the growth of data services follows its life-cycle curve.
