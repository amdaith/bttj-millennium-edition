Cost Apportionment

Established networks generally support many services. The question arises as to how the services should be charged for the use of the network? One way of apportioning costs to services is to say that each pays a proportion of the total costs of the network, in relation to its share of the total traffic on the network. This means that the more heavily a network is loaded, the more thinly the costs are spread, and the less any one individual call costs (figure 3).

Fig 3. A cost apportionment methodology

The total direct costs of the network include all capital expenditure and depreciation, non-recurring current account items, i.e. one-off payments, and maintenance expenditure on the components of the network. Total indirect costs have also to be included; there is a centralised mechanism for allocating these items to BT's various networks and platforms.

The unit cost of a call is effectively the sum of the fully allocated unit costs of all the components that are traced out in the call's path. The fully allocated costs include items such as depreciation of capital plant, the costs of people, power, lighting and ventilation, and also corporate overheads, such as the costs of company headquarters and public relations activities.

Wholesale unit costs are the network unit costs, while retail unit costs include an element representing the capital and current account expenditure of the customer-facing trading units in the company.
