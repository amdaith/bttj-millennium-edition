There's Nowt so Queer as Folk!

People will be people

People just can't stop being people - and people have a vast array of behaviours finely tuned over millions of years of social communication with other people. Spoken language for example is believed to have begun evolving among homosapiens at a date up to 1.5 million years ago [1]. Language is the essence of what makes us human, there is no other species that is able to use language to the same degree of complexity as mankind. Some species have shown considerable aptitude for learning language, for example the chimpanzee Washoe in the early 1970s learnt a vocabulary of about 130 words over a four year period. By contrast, a human child can learn 10 000 words in the same time, and during the 'critical period' where language development is at it's height, children can learn multiple languages with ease, a task that would prove difficult for many adults. Humans are predisposed for language, we have vocal chords, lips, tongue, soft palate that allow us to formulate the sounds for speech, and from birth our brain is able to distinguish between most of the phonemes that are used in languages across the world. And yet, we have created a world where the computer - a supportive aid to human intelligence, may only be used by entering its world of numbers and symbols, where we communicate our purposes with the precision of mathematics.

This is now changing. Computers are still programmed with precise mathematical languages, but the everyday applications which people use have interfaces which are much closer to our normal means of communication. Our instinctive behaviours are now being utilised by machines that respond to sound and touch, machines that let us be what we are - human. It has even been shown that interactive interfaces using the crudest of media, such as raw text, can unconsciously elicit social behaviours from people [2]. Technological sophistication can now let us input text by pen, electronically sensing movement to recognise handwriting, presenting a seamless interface between human and machine. Advanced speech recognisers exist that can listen to our requests, understand, and act on them. When people attempt to talk with computers they bring with them the same psychological limitations and social behaviours that they would to any ordinary conversation. In short - people just can't stop being people!
