Foreword

Peter Cochrane, Guest Editor of the BT Technology Journal

 

Our species is driven to communicate ideas and intentions in the richest of ways through gesture, speech, pictures, and latterly, the printed page. All have served us well, but more recently we have added moving pictures, animation and simulation to our armoury.

In a world of rapidly accelerating technology there is seldom a simple explanation and we have to realise greater speed and depth of understanding; we have to be able to see, hear, manipulate and experiment, in real-time. Looking back it is clear that professional journals have become staid and inadequate for the task at hand. So it is with great pleasure, along with my fellow editors, producers and contributing authors, that I welcome you to this millennial BTTJ issue. Being the very first of a new breed of technical journals you will find it full of novelty. We have enjoyed producing it, and we do hope you enjoy exploring it, and we look forward to your feedback as it evolves with the technology and people it serves.

Peter Cochrane

18:16, 22 November 99.

Typed on a lap top computer impossible to conceive of five years ago, and communicated over a network that did not exist 10 years ago, from a car with more electronics than the lunar-lander heading West on the M25.
