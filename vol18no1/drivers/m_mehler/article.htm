Mobility

Mick Mehler, Manager of Mobility & Network Services
 

From being the poor relation to the PSTN, cellular mobility has become one of the most significant growth areas in communications. Fifteen years ago, mobility meant just analogue voice, involving clumsy terminal devices with heavy batteries and operating on networks with only a few thousand users. Today, the digital GSM standard is well established, mobile networks support millions of users and terminal devices are both small and highly functional. Although mobile network traffic is still dominated by voice, the launch of general packet radio (GPRS) and 3rd generation systems is set to create a mobile datawave similar to that currently overwhelming fixed networks. Tomorrow, mobility will no longer be just voice, but will begin to embrace the rich set of IP applications seen on fixed networks.

ACE has been involved in enabling all stages of the mobility revolution. We were instrumental in the design and deployment of today's digital networks, both in the UK and in our many JVs. Our state-of-the-art ability to plan mobile networks to meet capacity targets within tight spectrum and infrastructure budgets, ensures that our skills are always in demand. The massive growth in mobility, with some pundits predicting that half the UK's voice calls will be mobile-originated by 2002, means that ACE has an on-going challenge to grow network capacity, while maintaining a quality of service which differentiates us from our competitors.

As we move into the next millennium, ACE will be instrumental in enabling the evolution towards the 3rd generation systems that are essential to create the mobile datawave. The 3rd generation vision exploits IP to realise multimedia services, which are accessible seamlessly across disparate networks and terminals. Here our biggest challenge is to understand how the new technology can come together with the market to create compelling service propositions that will fuel tomorrow's revenues. ACE is charged with charting a commercially viable course towards this seamless service vision.

Increasingly ACE's role will become more globally focused and as the GV becomes a reality, ACE will be central to realising the BT Group's vision of 'linking BT's and AT&T's mobile operations world-wide to develop the world's first global service'.
