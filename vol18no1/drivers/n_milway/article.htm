Global Ventures and Alliances

Nigel Milway , General Manager of Alliance Engineering
 

 

BT has long since extended its business beyond UK shores and is rapidly approaching the point where offshore Alliance revenues equal those in the UK.

It's also true that because the international businesses are younger, smaller and less regulated, they're able to move quickly into new technologies and markets. That offers useful experience to augment that gained in the UK.

The ACE thrust supports BT world-wide and the AT&T/BT Global Venture across the full range of communications technologies. It works in partnership with all the UK thrusts, seeking synergies where possible.

It's aimed at helping new businesses to get established quickly on a sound technical basis, and there are new successes every month in that respect.

Equally important, however, is the opportunity to share good design across the Alliance and to build on experience and technology from the UK.

In that way we not only reach markets more quickly and cheaply, but we also prepare to support global products and end-to-end customer service.

In their early stages, BT joint ventures are small and need to move quickly to gain market share. While there are notable exceptions, in general BTUK solutions are too large, inflexible and expensive for ventures to use. Generally the correct answer is to use proprietary solutions within a carefully designed framework.

Many ventures, however, are now growing in size, and in the areas supported by the leading edge technologies are becoming comparable to BT. In areas like data, IP telephony, ISP platforms, Internet applications, eBusiness, mobile (narrow and broadband), there are emerging opportunities to share designs and solutions.

The AT&T/BT Global Venture is critical here, both in its need to quickly establish a major Internet-based market presence, and the opportunity to focus convergence across BT UK and International.

It is on this opportunity that the International Thrust is focused.
