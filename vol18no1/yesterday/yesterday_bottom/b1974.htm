A Protocol for Packet Network Intercommunication

IEEE Transactions on Communications, Vol. COM-22, No 5, pp637-648, May 1974

V G Cerf and R E Kahn

Synopsis

Vint Cerf is known as the 'Father of the Internet'. He was responsible for the creation of the Internet TCP Protocol suite which transformed the ARPANET into a network of heterogeneous networks with computers on them which could interact using this set of protocols. Cerf and Bob Kahn described the method of constructing this heterogeneous network in 1973. Cerf led the group at Stanford University that produced the first TCP protocols in 1975 with implementation as testing of the TCP being done by University College London. Cerf joined ARPANET in 1976 and then, when the TCP/IP protocols were due to roll out of the research community, he left. Since then he has been active in the Internet Board, the Internet Society and the Internet Engineering Task Force.

Cerf, who was born in Connecticut in 1943, obtained his Bachelors degree from Stanford University and his Masters and Doctors from UCLA. He joined MCI in 1994 and is currently Senior Vice President for Internet Architecture and Technology. He is widely quoted for his visionary statement "Internet over everything and everything over Internet".
