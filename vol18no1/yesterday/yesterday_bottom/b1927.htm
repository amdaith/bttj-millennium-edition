Application of the Theory of Probability to Telephone Trunking

Bell System Technical Journal Vol.6, pp461-494, July 1927

Edward C Molina

Synopsis

Edward Molina entered the Research Department of AT&T in 1901. He was largely a self-taught mathematician, having never gone to university. His contributions, though in some respects predated by the work of the French mathematician Poisson, represented a fundamental contribution to the economics of dimensioning of switching systems and networks. His contributions extended beyond the field of probabilistic traffic analysis and, in particular, he is largely attributed with the invention of the concept of number translation. This not only yielded great cost savings through greater flexibility in plant provision but also set the scene for later techniques in early computing machine designs. Although others, such as A K Erlang of Denmark, made considerable contributions to the mathematical treatment of traffic, Molina remains widely regarded as a key contributor to the early understanding of the dimensioning of switching machines.

The current paper presents, in Molina's clear style, his analysis of trunking problems and the quantification of 'delay' effects. The paper presents case studies and many probabilistic trunking charts.
