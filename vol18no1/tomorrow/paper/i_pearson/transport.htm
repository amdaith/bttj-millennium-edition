BT Technology Timeline - Towards Life in 2020

Transport

As for human transport, we will need a fully integrated road traffic information and management system to cope with the extra load. Such systems can double the capacity of a road, while reducing the stress of travel. Our computer might negotiate the appropriate slots on the motorways to guarantee we get there on time, long before we leave, and will automatically re-route in case of problems en-route. All we have to do is sit back and watch as the car automatically takes us there. Many of our vehicles will be powered by non-polluting hydrogen fuel cells. However, with total world travel hitting 50 trillion passenger kilometres, and over 5 billion air travellers, some popular destinations may react by severely limiting the number of physical visitors, forcing the remainder to travel virtually on the net.

So the future looks different in many ways, but some things won't change. We will still have fundamentally the same needs and desires as the cavemen. We will still need food and shelter, still need love, still want some status and some self-fulfilment. And we will still squabble sometimes. These human attributes are written in our DNA, and while we might begin to tinker with that, some of this nature goes very deep indeed.
