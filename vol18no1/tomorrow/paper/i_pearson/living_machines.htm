BT Technology Timeline - Towards Life in 2020

Living with the machines

Full voice interaction with machines and other language applications such as translation have been promised for some time and always seem just on the horizon. Nevertheless, by 2020 we can expect to be able to converse with a smart machine about anything, and it will usually have the intelligence to implement appropriate requests. Computers are likely to have faces and personalities by then. Voice synthesis should sound perfectly natural.

The computers of 2020 will look unlike those today, in fact most of the time we won't see them. They will be ubiquitous but invisible, hidden in the infrastructure and in almost every device around us. They are likely to be at least a hundred thousand times faster than today's, maybe even a million times. Memory for computers will be in the multi-petabit range, more than the human brain. Storage will no longer be based on disks. Moving parts are just not needed and will disappear, replaced by holographic storage and a variety of other forms. We will still need some supercomputers, maybe to run the company board, and some of these may have 1000 million processors, some digital, some analog, some neural, some quantum and some using molecular computing techniques.

And of course, some of these computers will be mobile, on robots. They will have an array of sensors comparable or superior to anything in the natural world. In some countries, robot population will be approaching human population by 2020. We will use them for all manner of jobs around the office and home as well as in factories and agriculture. Millions of robots will be small and insect-like. There may be a flock of them maintaining the lawn and keeping the garden tidy, picking up individual leaves and carrying them to the compost heap. Some robots will act as pets and toys, others will take part in sports. We will not have androids like Data by 2020 but many robots will have some organic characteristics. Some might use muscles based on gels that resemble organic muscles. And imagine a robotic garden gnome that actually manages to catch the occasional fish in the garden pond, albeit a robotic fish.

In fact, cyberspace will increasingly affect every area of our lives, with a global Internet penetration of 75% by 2020. We will take access to the Net for granted wherever we are. Cordless communication will be the norm long before then, but the infrastructure will still be fibre, probably all the way to the home. However, capacity will be a problem, even on optical fibre. Demand is increasing very fast today and is likely to accelerate still faster in the future, but one day we will approach the maximum capacity of fibre, something we considered infinite not so long ago. We will have a multitude of wearable devices, not just watches and phones. Jewellery that reacts to our emotional state, audio and video devices, personality badges that advertise our presence to others of like mind, translators, and cameras built into our glasses to monitor whatever we see, are just a few examples. We might even have displays built into our clothes, but fashion is impossible to predict.
